2. Who leads the streaming shift?

Drawing on published survey responses from Australian adults, this section follows paid subscription streaming from 2017 to 2025. Figures show the percentage of each age group using a paid streaming service in the previous seven days.

Click the years below the bump chart to see the real values in the right bar chart.

Middle-aged adults lead in 2025

The 18–24 group moves from first place in 2017 to fourth in 2025, when 70.1% report using paid streaming. A lower rank does not necessarily mean their usage declined.

The youngest adults no longer rank first

Select a year to compare streaming use among younger and older Australians. Look at the percentages to see how far apart the age groups are.

A generation gap remains

In 2025, streaming use is 43.7% among people aged 75+, compared with 74.6% among 45–54-year-olds—a gap of 30.9 percentage points.

4. How Australians consume music

The shift to streaming extends beyond video. Compare free and paid music streaming across generations, then explore participation across states and territories.

Loading chart…

Loading chart…

Younger listeners favour paid streaming

Among 15–24-year-olds, 77.3% use paid music streaming, compared with 68.0% using free services.

Free streaming leads from age 35

Free streaming becomes more common than paid subscriptions among people aged 35 and over. For those aged 65+, free streaming reaches 39.1%, compared with 18.6% for paid services.

Music streaming varies across Australia

Participation ranges from 67.3% in the Northern Territory to 83.3% in the ACT—a difference of 16 percentage points in 2022.

6. Australia’s digital divide

Media habits describe what people use. Digital inclusion adds context about their opportunity to participate online, combining access, affordability and digital ability.

Loading chart…

The ACT leads digital inclusion

With a score of 79.5 out of 100, the ACT sits 6.0 points above the national average of 73.6.

Tasmania faces the largest gap

Tasmania records the lowest score at 69.4, around 10.1 points below the ACT.

Four jurisdictions fall below the national average

Queensland, South Australia, Tasmania and the Northern Territory score below 73.6, highlighting geographic differences in digital inclusion.
